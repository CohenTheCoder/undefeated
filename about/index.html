How it works

The games

  • 🏀 82-0: Point guard to center, one real season at a time. Win all 82. (Pro basketball, 1946-47 to 2025-26.)
  • 🏒 84-0: A center, both wingers, a left and right defenseman and a goalie. Win all 84. (Pro hockey, 1940-41 to 2025-26.)
  • 🏈 16-0: Twelve picks from quarterback to safety. Win all 16. (Pro football, 1940 to 2025.)
  • ⚾ 162-0: Nine bats, an ace and a closer. Win all 162. (Pro baseball incl. the Negro Leagues, 1940 to 2025.)
  • ⚽ 17-0: Pick your formation and fill it with European champions past and present. Win all 17. (Europe's top club competition, 1955-56 to 2025-26.)

Player ratings

Every player-season gets an overall rating from 40 to 99. We first compute a value for the season from real stats, then compare it with the regulars of the same season and position group. A typical starter lands around 72, a very good one in the 80s, and only historic seasons reach the high 90s. Because each player is graded against his own era, a 1950s star and a 2020s star are directly comparable.

  • Baseball: batting runs above average (linear weights), fielding runs (range and errors against others at his position), a positional adjustment and playing time, much like WAR. Starters and relievers are graded on runs saved against a replacement level, using a blend of ERA and FIP.
  • Basketball: mostly overall impact per game (points, rebounds, assists, steals, blocks, shooting efficiency and turnovers, the way Game Score and the efficiency rating add them up), with extra credit for carrying a team's scoring load and a smaller layer of position-specific attributes (point guards create, centers rebound and protect the rim). Missing big chunks of a season costs a little.
  • Hockey: scoring rate (goals count more than assists), plus-minus and ice time for skaters, graded separately for forwards and defensemen, with defensemen on a slightly narrower scale so a high-scoring blueliner doesn't outrank the league's top forwards; goals saved against an average goalie for goalies.
  • Football: from 1999 on, passing, rushing, receiving and defensive production by position, plus how often the player started. Before 1999 there are no open per-player stats, so ratings combine Pro Bowl and All-Pro honors, how widely documented the player is, and his team's point differential.
  • Club soccer: how far the club went that season, the player's appearances and goals per season at the club, his age, how widely documented his career is, and Ballon d'Or voting.

Positions

Players only fit positions they really played. Basketball splits the league's guard and forward labels into point and shooting guards, small and power forwards, using each season's assists, rebounds and blocks. Hockey separates left and right wingers, and left and right defensemen by the hand they shoot with. Football uses depth-chart positions (tackles, guards and centers, edge rushers and interior linemen, cornerbacks and safeties); before 1955, when most players went both ways, a player can fill a spot on either side of the ball. In club soccer you choose a formation, and full-backs, centre-backs, holding and attacking midfielders and wingers each have their own spots.

The simulation

Your team rating is a weighted average of your lineup. Each game is played against an opponent of typical playoff strength, and the chance of winning grows with the rating gap. The knobs are tuned on the real rosters so a typical draft wins about three games in four, while a perfect season stays a rare, shareable event. The odds shown after each draft are exact for that lineup.

Data sources and licenses

Only official, public-domain or openly licensed sources are used. Rosters run through the 2025-26 seasons.

  • Baseball: Lahman Baseball Database by Sean Lahman, licensedCC BY-SA 3.0. Our derived ratings are shared under the same license.
  • Football: nflverse rosters, player stats, depth charts and schedules (CC BY 4.0); historical game results from FiveThirtyEight (CC BY 4.0); Pro Bowl and All-Pro selections from Wikipedia (CC BY-SA 4.0); player coverage from Wikidata (CC0).
  • Hockey: public statistics from the league's stats service.
  • Basketball: public statistics from the league's stats service.
  • Club soccer: match results from engsoccerdata andopenfootball (CC0); squads, positions, heights, appearances and goals from Wikidata (CC0); Ballon d'Or voting from Wikipedia (CC BY-SA 4.0).

Spotted a wrong rating or a missing player? Ratings are generated from data, so mistakes in the sources can slip through. Let us know and we'll fix the source or the formula.

Not affiliated

Undefeated is an independent fan game. It is not affiliated with, endorsed or sponsored by any league, team, players' association or player. Team and player names are used only to identify historical seasons, and no logos or likenesses are used.
